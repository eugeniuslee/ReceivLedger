## Wallet switching fixes

- BrowserProvider accepts network changes (`any`) and disables its short request cache, avoiding stale account/chain responses around switches.
- An identity epoch advances for wallet/network/disconnect transitions. It invalidates private registration and pinned financial contexts even if React batches A?B?A into a render with the original address.
- Generation/sequence checks discard out-of-order contract, history, private-session and financial responses.
- Transaction preflight requires the first selected authorised account, not merely an old account still present in MetaMask's authorised-account array.
- Empty authorised accounts clear the identity/balances and restore Connect Wallet. Passive synchronisation does not request permission or signatures. A same-wallet notification retains a valid matching private session; a real identity change requires that wallet's own private authentication.
- The app cannot infer a hidden selected account if a wallet exposes neither an accounts event nor changed authorised accounts. It does not circumvent MetaMask permissions or warnings.

## Independent V2 contract

`contracts/ReceivLedgerV2.sol` uses OpenZeppelin ERC20, Ownable2Step, Pausable and Math.mulDiv. RLU2 has **2 decimals**; one raw token unit initially represents one simulated SGD cent. V1 RLU has zero decimals and is not interchangeable with RLU2. No function is payable and there are no external asset transfers/calls. Reentrancy protection would need reassessment if external payment/token calls were introduced; there are none in this simulated accounting design.

The primary owner verifies investors/SMEs, confirms simulated funding/collections, reviews larger requests, records valuations/impairments and controls operators. Ownership transfer needs acceptance; renunciation is blocked. Executors and independent invoice attestors are separately granted/revoked. An executor cannot attest funding, mark participants verified, change policy, approve larger requests or approve a redemption for the first time. An SME cannot attest its own invoice even if granted an attestor permission. Emergency pause stops new subscriptions/financing, while collections, cancellation and allocated redemption settlement remain possible.

Automation starts disabled. Manager-configurable automatic thresholds may be reduced, but cannot exceed S$10,000 subscriptions or S$5,000 invoices. Optional additional overdue interest is disabled by default and capped at 3%; changes apply only to new invoices with explicitly accepted terms.

### Subscriptions

An approved investor requests an amount and minimum units with a unique nonce. The owner must attest a unique simulated funding reference; a wallet signature never means funding arrived. Pending confirmed funding is segregated outside NAV and fund liquidity. Above S$10,000 lifetime cumulatively processed automatically across an investor identity needs owner review; smaller confirmed requests may enter a restricted executor queue. Processing rechecks investor approval, the fund ceiling, valuation and minimum units, then atomically books funds and mints once. Cancellation is allowed before funding. Rejected funded requests become Refund Pending until an owner records their simulated refund; they never silently disappear.

The S$10,000,000 maximum is enforced against fund assets including reserved redemption liabilities when **admitting capital**. Recognising earned income may take NAV above that ceiling; it is not erased. New subscriptions then remain blocked. The new contract starts with zero capital, not S$10 million automatically.

### Units, NAV and redemption

Fund assets = fund liquidity + unpaid gross loan principal + earned unpaid interest - recorded impairment.
Active NAV = fund assets ? allocated redemption liabilities.
Pricing units = total ERC20 supply ? units committed to allocated redemptions.

Earned unpaid interest is included in investment NAV and is removed from the receivable when collected, without recognising income twice. Future interest, pending subscriptions and SME reserves are excluded. The current review documents commitment reservations, expected losses and bounded live accrual. Initially, with zero NAV and zero units, one RLU2 is S$1. Subsequently:

- Subscription raw units = floor(confirmed cents ? pricing units / active NAV).
- Redemption cents = floor(requested raw units ? active NAV / pricing units).

No stale par price is substituted. Outstanding financing requires a manager-confirmed valuation valid for at most 24 hours. Financing, collections and impairment changes invalidate it. Zero NAV with outstanding units blocks pricing. No oracle proves the loans' fair value: the owner's external impairment/valuation review is an explicit trust assumption, including overdue loans. Realised charges change the price; losses reduce it.

Redemption requests escrow units immediately, preventing transfer or double redemption. All require manager review. If insufficient unreserved liquidity exists, they remain Pending Liquidity: no fixed payout or cash claim is reserved yet. Later allocation reprices against current NAV and the user's minimum payout. Fully allocated requests lock their payout and reserve cash; their units/liability are excluded from ongoing pricing together. Settlement burns those escrowed units and consumes only reserved simulated cash. Pending requests may be cancelled/rejected; an allocated commitment cannot be cancelled/rejected at an obsolete price. Nonapproved investors retain public holdings/history and can cancel an unallocated exit, but cannot request a new exit; ordinary transfers still require approved counterparties. No SGD payment occurs.

Rounding is conservative to cents/raw units. Subscription minimum units and redemption minimum cents protect the user's accepted limit. Tiny positions may need aggregation because zero-unit or zero-cent settlement is blocked. These are neither guaranteed returns nor real-money withdrawal claims.

### SME policy and evidence

The S$100,000 hard cap applies to aggregate **unpaid gross principal plus approved gross commitments per verified entity**, across all approved wallets, not separately per wallet. The owner assigns a canonical opaque entity ID; a wallet's entity binding is permanent. Submitted invoices bind that entity and cannot finance under a different later binding. Per-wallet exposure is retained for reconciliation, along with aggregate entity and canonical payer exposure.

Up to S$5,000: 100% gross advance. Above S$5,000: 90% gross advance and mandatory manager review. Exceeding 1% of current active fund NAV or non-low risk also requires review. Eligible small invoices can become **AutoApproved** only through an authorised executor/owner; approval reserves net liquidity, gross entity/payer capacity and automatic facility capacity where applicable. Automatic financing also requires the S$50,000 automatic facility and 30-day maturity limits. Manual review permits at most 90 days. At or above 1% NAV, explicit invoice-specific Manager acknowledgement is required; above 1% cannot be automatic. Manual review remains the V2 Submitted state until approved or rejected; the UI policy label Pending Manager Review is not a new V1 status.

Every approval/financing checks verified entity/wallet, independently attested authenticity and credit clearance, future maturity within 90 days, the entity borrowing cap, 10% canonical payer concentration and unreserved liquidity. Financing rechecks current constraints and automatic eligibility. Only one financing per invoice is possible. Explicit manager rejection records a reason and a real V2 Rejected state. V1 rejection/accounting is not altered.

Document digests and canonical invoice references are unique across submissions. This blocks exact replay, not all fraud: changing a PDF or reference can change its hash. Independent review must bind genuine canonical invoice/payer/entity identifiers and detect near-duplicates. No PII/PDF is stored in the contract. Never derive public entity IDs directly from predictable private identifiers without reviewing privacy; maintain an opaque registry mapping off-chain.

### Fees, collections and reserves

Processing fee S$20 is withheld once from the gross advance. Net upfront simulated proceeds = gross advance ? S$20. The fee is recognised as retained financing income; it is not added to debt again. Amounts that cannot produce positive upfront proceeds are rejected.

Base interest is 8% simple annual interest on unpaid gross principal. The contract uses elapsed seconds / (365 days), avoiding repeated day rounding. Integer-cent interest is floored with numerator remainders carried across partial collections. No interest-on-interest. Any final sub-cent residue is waived when the debt closes. The optional additional overdue rate accrues only after maturity, with the loan's accepted rate fixed at submission. Early repayment penalty is zero.

Owner-confirmed payer and SME collections share one debt ledger and unique receipt references. Collections pay accrued base interest, overdue interest, then principal. Paid principal reduces wallet/entity/payer exposure and outstanding assets exactly once. A full face-value payer collection does not justify charging that principal again to the SME. An SME early payoff followed by payer collection creates SME surplus rather than double fund income. Partial collections can leave principal/interest still due.

Payer surplus is segregated as SME reserve payable, outside fund liquidity/NAV. It can be released only after the debt is fully Repaid; releases are single-use. The original 10% holdback is not automatically a fee or an asset owned by the fund. Actual reserve release depends on collections, interest and any earlier SME payments.

Examples at 30 whole days, with no prior collections or overdue surcharge:

| Face | Gross | Upfront after S$20 | Interest | Debt before payer offset | Outcome after full face collection |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| S$5,000 | S$5,000 | S$4,980 | S$32.87 | S$5,032.87 | SME interest top-up S$32.87 |
| S$10,000 | S$9,000 | S$8,980 | S$59.17 | S$9,059.17 | Unused reserve S$940.83 payable to SME |

The real debt uses execution timestamps and actual unpaid principal; quotations are estimates. All amounts are simulated.

## Execution and Supabase activation design

The on-chain request states are the secure execution queue: request IDs and terminal status checks prevent repeated processing even with competing workers. A future worker may call only processSubscription, approveSmallInvoice, financeInvoice on eligible/reviewed loans, allocate previously reviewed redemptions, and settle already allocated redemptions. It must re-read the exact chain/address/request state before execution, check automation/pause/valuation and persist the resulting receipt. Contract conditions remain authoritative if state races. The owner retains funding confirmations, participant verification and first redemption/large-request reviews.

No worker/key store/scheduler is provisioned. An approved executor should be an independently managed signer with narrowly scoped on-chain powers and monitoring, not the primary manager's key. Primary manager keys must never be stored in frontend code, Vercel or Supabase secrets. Granting, hosting and funding any operator require separate approval. No unattended timing or guaranteed processing is promised.

Existing registry is V1-specific and must not be silently pointed at V2. The isolated local `registry-v2` handler and `supabase/v2/registration.sql` draft now implement the registration gate described in the current review. They remain undeployed/unapplied. Document handling, live forms and worker integration still require review and approval. Original backend design context:

1. A separate version-scoped registration/attestation service verifies Supabase Auth, the active Ethereum identity/session and live V2 owner/operator permissions. Existing V1 approval alone is not a V2 role. Reusing fictional KYC requires explicit provenance/consent and review, not automatic on-chain grants.
2. Version-scoped verification records bind chain ID, V2 address, wallet, opaque entity, source application version, reviewer, status and confirmed verification transaction. Canonical payer/invoice/document reviews are bound to the precise invoice ID and terms. Private records remain owner/authorised reviewer-only under new reviewed RLS; do not widen the existing V1 document policies.
3. Any durable off-chain job records use a unique chain/address/action/request-ID key, expected state/version, expiry, attempts and receipt, with owner/operator-only execution. Client-visible job status must not imply chain success without a verified receipt. Service-role credentials never enter the browser.
4. Different V1/V2 owner wallets require explicit document access policy design. V2 owner labels must not impersonate the current V1 manager. A new function, migration or operator deployment remains subject to separate approval; none was applied/prepared as a secretly executable hosted change.

The current preview provides a quotation, real future public V2 reads and disabled action affordances. The signer adapter is prepared and refuses V1 addresses, zero addresses and non-Sepolia execution. Full live wallet forms, private V2 onboarding, review screens and worker activation remain gated on the above decisions. This is a precise activation design, not a claim that hosted V2 automation currently works.

## V1-to-V2 migration proposal

Prefer parallel operation with an empty V2 fund. Preserve V1's read-only history, balances and obligations on their original chain/address; keep its existing UI accessible. Treat RLU2 as a distinct token/fund with its own explicitly confirmed simulated subscriptions. Do not mint V2 balances from V1 holdings or combine NAVs automatically.

If migration is later desired: inventory V1 holdings/approvals, outstanding loans, liquidity and records at a fixed finalised block; reconcile an independently reviewed opening valuation and liabilities; obtain holder opt-in and review a dedicated lock/claim or conversion contract. V1 has no general redemption/burn migration mechanism, so a frontend transfer is not a safe conversion. Establish custody, double-claim prevention, rollback, tax/legal scope if ever applicable, and whether claims consume V2 capital headroom. Each migration transaction, new contract and any private-data transfer requires separate approval. No balances are migrated by this implementation.

## Security and operational limitations

This is an unaudited university prototype, not a commercial finance system. Simulated receipt truth, authenticity, canonical entities/payers, credit risk, impairment and valuation depend on trusted review. No real payment rail, document fraud detector, credit oracle, production scheduler or hosted V2 registration is activated. No external call means no current reentrancy surface through fund transfers, but any real asset integration changes that assessment. Public loan hashes and balances require privacy review before any real data use.

The compiler's newly encountered tmp advisory was resolved using a scoped patched tmp 0.2.7 override. Current production dependency audit: zero advisories. Six pre-existing development-tool advisories remain (3 moderate, 1 high, 2 critical across the existing Vite/Vitest toolchain); no force/major upgrade was applied to V1. They need a separate compatibility/security upgrade before claiming deployment readiness. Solidity tests execute bytecode in a disposable in-memory EthereumJS EVM with no network provider, wallet keys, signed transactions or public-chain writes. OpenZeppelin patterns are documented at https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/api/token/erc20 and https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/api/access; the local VM API is from https://github.com/ethereumjs/ethereumjs-monorepo.

## Approval gates

Before any deployment: independent contract/security/accounting review; resolution/review of existing tooling advisories; valuation, reserve, rounding, accepted overdue pricing and simulation terms; choice of V2 manager/attestors/executor custody; implementation/review of V2 private onboarding and interactive wallet UI; explicit chain/address deployment approval. Hosted migrations/functions/settings, operator activation, commits/push/merges, Vercel publication and any legacy conversion each require separate approval. No new deployment address or hosted settings are assumed.
